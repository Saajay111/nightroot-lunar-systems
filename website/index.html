A DIFFERENT PERSPECTIVE

What if the answer
was already underfoot?

On the Moon, surviving the long night is a major challenge for surface equipment. NightRoot explores a simple but ambitious idea: instead of protecting every system only from the surface, move selected equipment beneath it — and investigate the protective role of surrounding lunar regolith.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

A harsh environment.
A demanding mission.

Long-duration lunar operations require systems that can withstand extended periods without sunlight and extreme environmental conditions.

01 — LUNAR NIGHT
~14 Earth days

A long period in darkness

A lunar night lasts roughly two Earth weeks at locations that experience a normal lunar day-night cycle. Without sunlight, surface systems face a difficult power and thermal environment.

02 — THERMAL MANAGEMENT
◌

Protecting sensitive systems

Electronics, batteries, and scientific instruments must operate within their allowable temperature ranges. Thermal protection can add mass, power demand, and design complexity.

03 — MISSION RESILIENCE
⌁

Making every kilogram count

Lunar payloads face strict constraints. New protection strategies must be evaluated not only for performance, but also for mass, energy, reliability, and integration.

Background: NASA — Moon. Conditions vary by location, terrain, and mission design.

NRT—01 / SYSTEM CONCEPT

THE CONCEPT

Move critical technology below the surface.

NightRoot is designed around a proposed dual counter-rotating auger arrangement intended to help position a compact pod within lunar regolith. The surrounding soil would then act as an additional passive thermal barrier.

01

Deploy

The system is placed on the lunar surface.

02

Burrow

The proposed auger assembly is intended to move the pod into the regolith.

03

Position

The current concept targets a subsurface depth of approximately 30 cm.

04

Protect

Surrounding regolith may provide passive thermal protection; performance requires testing.

DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Make the environment part of the solution.

NightRoot investigates whether subsurface placement can complement conventional thermal-management approaches. The engineering challenge is to determine whether the mechanical system can deploy reliably and whether the resulting thermal benefit justifies its mass, energy, and complexity.
